## A. THE FRAME — कथा कौन, किसे, कहाँ सुना रहा है

### A1. Naimiṣa forest (1.1–1.2)
Ugraśravas Sauti, son of Lomaharṣaṇa, arrives at Kulapati Śaunaka's twelve-year sacrificial session in the Naimiṣa forest. The sages ask for the story he heard at King Janamejaya's snake sacrifice from Vaiśaṁpāyana, Vyāsa's disciple. Sauti gives a table of contents (Parvasaṁgraha, 1.2).
`[NOT CE]` Gaṇeśa writing the epic as Vyāsa's scribe — not in the constituted text (Brahmā's visit to Vyāsa = App. I.1; the Gaṇeśa lines are not even in the CE apparatus).

### A2. Why the snake sacrifice? (1.3–1.53)
* **Paushya / Uttaṅka (1.3):** Janamejaya conquers Takṣaśilā (1.3.18). The young sage Uttaṅka, robbed of a queen's earrings by the serpent Takṣaka, comes to Hastinapura and finds Janamejaya "just returned victorious from Takṣaśilā" (1.3.179). He tells him: *Takṣaka killed your father — avenge him* (1.3.185).
* **Parīkṣit's death (1.36–1.40):** Parīkṣit (Arjuna's grandson) insults a meditating sage by draping a dead snake on his shoulder; the sage's son Śṛṅgin curses him to die by Takṣaka's bite in seven days; despite precautions Takṣaka reaches him and burns him.
* **Āstīka's birth (1.13–1.48):** the serpents' mother Kadrū had cursed her own sons to burn in a king's fire; to save them, the sage Jaratkāru marries the serpent-king Vāsuki's sister and fathers Āstīka.
* **The sacrifice (1.47):** priests measure the ground; a wise architect-bard (sūtradhāra) warns: *"because of a brahmin this rite will not be completed"* (1.47.14–15). Priests in **black robes, eyes red with smoke**, chant; serpents — white, black, blue, old and young — scream and fall into the fire by hundreds of thousands (1.47.18–25).
* **Āstīka (1.49–1.53):** the boy praises the king and asks a boon: stop the sacrifice. Takṣaka hangs in the air above the flames; the boon is granted; the serpents are freed (1.53).

### A3. Vyāsa arrives; the story begins (1.54–1.57)
When Janamejaya was consecrated (dīkṣita) for the sacrifice, Kṛṣṇa Dvaipāyana Vyāsa arrives (1.54.1) — born to the fisher-girl Kālī (Satyavatī) and Parāśara on a Yamunā island (1.54.2). Janamejaya asks: **"How did the split arise among those of unblemished deeds? How did that great war, the destroyer of beings, happen?"** (1.54.18–20). Vyāsa tells Vaiśaṁpāyana: *tell him all you heard from me* (1.54.21–22). The epic calls itself **Jaya** (1.56.19), 100,000 verses (1.56.13), composed by Vyāsa "rising daily for three years" (1.56.32): *what is here is elsewhere; what is not here is nowhere* (1.56.33).
**Frame note for film:** the narration happens "in the intervals of the sacrificial rites" (18.5.26) at **Takṣaśilā** (18.5.29). Āstīka's freeing of the serpents is restated at the very end (18.5.27) — use as the trilogy's bookend.

### A4. The burden of the earth & partial incarnations (1.58–1.61)
After Paraśurāma's destruction of the Kṣatriyas, the earth is overrun by demons born as kings; the gods descend in portions. Selected `[CE]` identifications: **Duryodhana = portion of Kali** (1.61.80); **Śakuni = Dvāpara** (1.61.72); Bhīṣma = the Vasu Dyaus; Droṇa = from Bṛhaspati; Karṇa = from Sūrya; Vidura = Dharma (via the Māṇḍavya curse, 1.101); Kṛṣṇa = Nārāyaṇa; Balarāma = Śeṣa.

## B. THE LINEAGE (1.62–1.90) — film usage: 60-second animated-fresco montage only
Duṣyanta–Śakuntalā → **Bharata** (the land and dynasty named after him, 1.62–1.69) · Yayāti, Devayānī, Śarmiṣṭhā, the son Puru who takes his father's old age (1.70–1.80) · Kuru (Kurukṣetra) · … · **Pratīpa → Śaṁtanu**. ## C. GANGĀ, DEVAVRATA & THE VOW (1.91–1.96)

### C1. Gaṅgā (1.91–1.93)
The eight Vasus, cursed by Vasiṣṭha to be born as men, beg Gaṅgā to be their mother and drown them at birth so their human life ends instantly. Gaṅgā marries King Śaṁtanu on one condition: *"If you ever stop me or speak harshly, I will leave"* (1.92.35). Seven sons — she throws each into the river saying **"prīṇāmi tvām"** ("I gladden you") (1.92.44). At the eighth, as she seems to smile, the grieving king cries: **"Don't kill him! Who are you, whose are you? Why do you kill your sons? Child-killer, you incur great sin!"** (1.92.46–47). She reveals herself as Gaṅgā; the condition is broken; the eighth son (who must live long on earth because of Vasiṣṭha's curse, 1.93.42) she **takes away with her** (1.93.43).

### C2. The boy who held back the Ganga (1.94.18–1.94.40)
Śaṁtanu lives **36 years** (16+8+4+8) without women, wandering forests (1.94.18). One day, following a deer along the Gaṅgā, he finds the river reduced to a trickle: a handsome youth, wielding a divine weapon "like Indra", **has dammed the entire Ganga with sharp arrows** (1.94.21–25). Śaṁtanu doesn't recognise his own son; the youth bewilders him with māyā and vanishes (1.94.26–27). Gaṅgā appears holding the adorned boy by the right hand: he learned the Vedas from Vasiṣṭha, the śāstras of Śukra and Bṛhaspati, and the weapons of Paraśurāma — *"take him home"* (1.94.29–36). He is named **Devavrata**, also called Gāṅgeya (1.93.44). Śaṁtanu crowns him **Yuvarāja** (1.94.38). Four happy years pass (1.94.40).
> **Age note:** Devavrata is ≈36 when returned and ≈40 at the vow — a man in his prime, not a teenager.

### C3. Satyavatī (1.94.41–1.94.53)
Hunting near the Yamunā, Śaṁtanu smells an indescribable fragrance and finds a dark-eyed fisher-maiden who ferries a boat "for dharma, at her father's command" (1.94.41–44). Her father, the **chief of the fishermen (dāśarāja)**, sets a condition: *her son must be king after you* (1.94.51). Śaṁtanu refuses (he has Devavrata), returns home and wastes away with longing (1.94.52–53).

### C4. The terrible vow (1.94.54–1.94.94)
Devavrata asks his father; Śaṁtanu evades ("you are my only son; one son is no son; a warrior dies by weapons…", 1.94.57–63). Devavrata learns the truth from an **old minister** (1.94.65–66), goes **with elder Kṣatriyas** to the dāśarāja and asks for the girl for his father (1.94.67). The dāśa receives him formally "in the royal assembly" (1.94.68): the girl was born from the seed of a noble king (Uparicara Vasu, 1.94.71); he refused even the sage Asita (1.94.73); his only fear is *rivalry with you* (1.94.74–76).
* In the hearing of the assembled kings, Devavrata renounces the throne: *her son will be our king* (1.94.77–79).
* The dāśa: *but your sons…?* (1.94.84).
* Devavrata: **"Hear me, king of the fishermen, in the hearing of these kings: I gave up the kingdom already; now for offspring too I make this resolve — from today, O dāśa, I shall live in brahmacarya; even sonless, imperishable worlds in heaven will be mine."** (1.94.86–88)
* The dāśa, hair on end, says *"I give her"* (1.94.89). **Apsarases, gods and hosts of sages rain flowers and cry "This is Bhīṣma!"** (1.94.90).
* Devavrata turns to Satyavatī: **"Mount the chariot, mother; let us go home"** (1.94.91), brings her to Hastinapura (1.94.92). The kings praise the deed: "bhīṣmo 'yam" (1.94.93). **Śaṁtanu grants him death at will (svacchanda-maraṇa)** (1.94.94).

### C5. Citrāṅgada, Vicitravīrya, the Kāśi princesses (1.95–1.96)
Satyavatī bears Citrāṅgada and Vicitravīrya. Śaṁtanu dies. Citrāṅgada is killed by a gandharva of the same name before reaching youth (1.95). Bhīṣma rules as regent for the boy Vicitravīrya, then abducts the three princesses of Kāśi — Ambā, Ambikā, Ambālikā — from their swayamvara, defeating all kings including Śālva (1.96). Ambā says she had chosen Śālva; Bhīṣma lets her go (her tragedy returns in Udyoga 5.170–193 as Śikhaṇḍin's origin). Vicitravīrya marries the other two and, after seven years of pleasure, dies of consumption, childless (1.96).

## D. NIYOGA, THREE PRINCES, TWO QUEENS (1.97–1.106)
* **Satyavatī begs Bhīṣma** to father children on the widows or take the throne; Bhīṣma refuses his vow (1.97). Satyavatī reveals her premarital son **Vyāsa** and summons him (1.99).
* **Niyoga (1.100):** Ambikā shuts her eyes at the dark, ascetic Vyāsa → **Dhṛtarāṣṭra, born blind** "through his mother's fault" but destined for a hundred sons. Ambālikā turns pale → **Pāṇḍu** (pale). Sent a third time, Ambikā instead dresses her **maidservant in her own ornaments** (1.100.23) → **Vidura**, wise, an incarnation of Dharma.
* **Māṇḍavya (1.101):** why Dharma was born as a śūdra-woman's son — the sage Māṇḍavya, impaled for a theft he never committed, cursed Dharma for punishing a child's sin too harshly.
* **Pāṇḍu crowned** (the blind cannot rule) (1.102). Vidura becomes counsellor.
* **Gāndhārī (1.103):** she had received from Śiva a boon of a hundred sons (1.103.9). Her father Subala hesitates over the blind groom but agrees (1.103.11). Hearing her husband is blind, **"she took a cloth band, folded it many times, and bound her own eyes, devoted to her husband — resolving 'I will not surpass my husband'"** (1.103.13). Her brother **Śakuni brings her to the Kurus and returns home** (1.103.14–15).
* **Kuntī & Karṇa (1.104):** Pṛthā, daughter of the Yādava Śūra (Vasudeva's sister), is given in adoption to King Kuntibhoja. Serving the fierce sage **Durvāsas**, she receives a mantra to summon any god for a son "for a time of distress" (1.104.6). Out of curiosity she invokes the Sun (1.104.8). **Karṇa is born wearing natural armour (kavaca) and earrings (kuṇḍala)** (1.104.10–11). Sūrya restores her maidenhood (1.104.12). **Afraid of her kin, she casts him into the water** (1.104.13). The charioteer Adhiratha and his wife Rādhā adopt him, name him **Vasuṣeṇa** (1.104.14–15). He worships the Sun until his back burns and refuses nothing to brahmins during his prayers (1.104.16–17). *(Basket detail is in Vana Parva 3.292: a well-padded, lidded basket set afloat on the **Aśva** river → Carmaṇvatī → Yamunā → Gaṅgā → Campā.)*
* **Pāṇḍu's marriages & conquests (1.105–1.106):** Kuntī chooses Pāṇḍu at her swayamvara; Bhīṣma buys Mādrī, Śalya's sister, for Pāṇḍu; Pāṇḍu conquers kingdoms and gives the wealth to the Kurus, then retires to the forest with both wives. Vidura marries.

## E. BIRTHS (1.107–1.118)
* **Gāndhārī's pregnancy (1.107):** two years pregnant; hearing that Kuntī has borne a son "bright as the morning sun", she **strikes her own womb in secret**; an iron-hard ball of flesh emerges (1.107.9–12). Vyāsa: *"my word is never false"* — a hundred **ghee-filled pots**, cool water; the mass splits into **101** thumb-sized parts (1.107.17–21). **Duryodhana** is born first among them; Yudhiṣṭhira is the eldest of all (1.107.24). As Dhṛtarāṣṭra asks the brahmins whether this son too will be king, **jackals and carrion-beasts howl in every direction** (1.107.28); the brahmins and Vidura say: *this son will destroy the family — abandon him* (1.107.30). Dhṛtarāṣṭra cannot. (Also: **Yuyutsu**, son of Dhṛtarāṣṭra by a vaiśya maid; **Duḥśalā**, the only daughter, from the 101st part.)
* **Kindama (1.109):** Pāṇḍu shoots a mating deer — the sage Kindama in deer-form — and is cursed: union with a wife will kill him. He renounces kingship and lives as an ascetic at Śataśṛṅga (1.110).
* **Kuntī's mantra (1.111–1.115):** at Pāṇḍu's urging Kuntī invokes **Dharma → Yudhiṣṭhira**; **Vāyu → Bhīma** — the day Bhīma is born, a startled Kuntī drops him off her lap and **the infant shatters the rock into a hundred pieces** (1.114.11–13); **on that same day Duryodhana is born** (1.114.14); **Indra → Arjuna**, with a celestial voice; Mādrī invokes the **Aśvins → Nakula & Sahadeva** (1.115.16–17).
* **Pāṇḍu's death (1.116):** in spring, overcome by desire for Mādrī in a lonely grove, he dies in her arms. Kuntī wishes to follow him; Mādrī insists, entrusts her twins to Kuntī, and **mounts his pyre** (1.116.31).
* **The rishis' testimony (1.117):** the Śataśṛṅga ascetics escort Kuntī, the five boys and **the two bodies** to Hastinapura **seventeen days after Pāṇḍu's death** (1.117.27–30), declare the boys Pāṇḍu's sons by the gods — and **vanish in an instant like a gandharva-city** (1.117.32–33).
* **Funeral (1.118):** royal cremation of Pāṇḍu and Mādrī by the Gaṅgā.

> **Continuity note:** the CE contains both Mādrī's pyre in the forest (1.116.31) and the "two bodies" brought home (1.117.30, 1.118). Film solution: show Mādrī stepping into the forest pyre (seen in fragments, smoke), then the rishis arriving with **two shrouded biers** of the remains.

## F. BOYHOOD, POISON, TEACHERS (1.119–1.128)
* **Satyavatī's exit (1.119.5–12):** after the śrāddha Vyāsa warns his mother: *"the happy times are gone; terrible times come; go to the forest so you don't see your family's destruction."* Satyavatī, Ambikā and Ambālikā go to the forest, do fierce austerity and die.
* **Bhīma the bully (1.119.14–23):** he drags the Kaurava boys by their feet, scraping knees and faces, holds ten of them under water until half-dead, kicks trees so they fall with the fruit — "out of childishness, not malice."
* **Three murder attempts (1.119.24–43):** Duryodhana resolves to kill Bhīma by deceit and imprison Yudhiṣṭhira and Arjuna. At **Pramāṇakoṭi** (riverside pavilions of cloth and blankets) Bhīma, exhausted and stupefied, sleeps; Duryodhana **binds him with creepers and throws him into the deep river — Bhīma wakes, snaps the bonds and climbs out** (1.119.34–35). Snakes bite him in his sleep — **their fangs cannot pierce his skin**; he crushes them and kills **Duryodhana's favourite charioteer** with a backhand (1.119.36–38). **Kālakūṭa** poison is put in his food — **Yuyutsu secretly warns the Pāṇḍavas**; Bhīma eats it and digests it unharmed (1.119.39–41). Duryodhana, Karṇa and Śakuni try many such ways; the Pāṇḍavas, on Vidura's advice, say nothing (1.119.42–43). `[NOT CE]` Nāgaloka, Vāsuki, Āryaka, nectar bowls = App. I.72–73.
* **Kṛpa & Droṇa (1.120–1.122):** Kṛpa and Kṛpī are born in a clump of reeds from the sage Śaradvat's seed and raised by Śaṁtanu; Kṛpa becomes the princes' first teacher. **Droṇa** is born in a vessel (droṇa) from Bharadvāja's seed; childhood friend of Prince **Drupada** of Pāñcāla, who promised to share his kingdom. Poor, Droṇa marries Kṛpī; their son **Aśvatthāman** neighs like a horse at birth. Drupada, now king, rejects him: *"a king cannot be friend to a pauper."* Droṇa comes to Hastinapura, impresses the princes (retrieves their ball from a well) and Bhīṣma appoints him guru.
* **Ekalavya (1.123.10–39):** Ekalavya, son of the Niṣāda king **Hiraṇyadhanus**, is refused by Droṇa "because he is a Niṣāda" (1.123.11). He touches Droṇa's feet, makes a **clay image** of Droṇa in the forest and masters archery, including shooting by sound (śabdavedhitva). On a royal hunt, a dog barks at this dark, mud-smeared, deerskin-clad stranger; Ekalavya fills its mouth with **seven arrows at once** (1.123.18–19). **Arjuna privately reproaches Droṇa: "You promised no pupil of yours would surpass me!"** (1.123.26–28). Droṇa takes Arjuna and goes to Ekalavya (1.123.29), asks his fee: **the right thumb** (1.123.35). Ekalavya, "with a cheerful face, undejected mind," cuts it off without hesitation (1.123.37). Afterwards he draws with his fingers and is no longer as fast (1.123.38). **"Arjuna was pleased, his fever gone"** (1.123.39).
* **The bird test (1.123.46–67):** an **artificial bhāsa bird made by craftsmen** is placed on a treetop; the target is its head (1.123.46–48). Yudhiṣṭhira first: "I see the tree, you, my brothers, the bird" — dismissed. Duryodhana, Bhīma and princes from other lands give the same answer. Arjuna: **"I see only the bird."** — "Describe it." — **"I see its head, not its body"** (1.123.62–64). He shoots without hesitation; a razor-headed arrow (kṣura) brings the head down (1.123.66). `[NOT CE]` "the eye of the bird."
* **The crocodile (1.123.68–77):** a crocodile seizes Droṇa's shank in the Gaṅgā; the others freeze; Arjuna kills it with five arrows. Reward: the **brahmaśiras** weapon — *never to be used on humans; the world would burn* (1.123.74–77).
* **The tournament & Karṇa (1.124–1.127):** see screenplay; key beats: Karṇa enters through a crowd that parts for him (1.126.1), bows perfunctorily to Droṇa and Kṛpa (1.126.6), challenges Arjuna "brother to the unknown brother" (1.126.8), repeats all his feats; Duryodhana embraces him, Karṇa asks only for **friendship and a duel with Arjuna** (1.126.15); thunderclouds shade Arjuna, the Sun clears the sky over Karṇa (1.126.23–25); **Kuntī recognises him and faints; Vidura revives her with sandal-water** (1.126.27–28); Kṛpa demands his lineage (1.126.30–32); Karṇa's face droops "like a rain-wet lotus" (1.126.33); Duryodhana: *three sources of kingship — birth, valour, leading armies* — and **anoints Karṇa King of Aṅga on the spot** (1.126.34–36); "what can I give you?" — **"lasting friendship"** (1.126.38). Then **Adhiratha** stumbles in; Karṇa bows his coronation-wet head; the old man drenches it with tears (1.127.1–4). **Bhīma: "Take up the whip, suta's son — you are no more fit for Aṅga than a dog for the sacrificial cake"** (1.127.6–7). Karṇa's lip trembles; he looks at the Sun (1.127.8). **Duryodhana: "the origins of heroes and rivers are hard to know… how could a doe give birth to a tiger?"** (1.127.11–17). Sunset; torches (1.127.18–19). **Kuntī feels a hidden joy** (1.127.22). Yudhiṣṭhira thinks: *no archer on earth equals Karṇa* (1.127.24).
* **Gurudakṣiṇā (1.128):** the pupils capture Drupada; Droṇa mocks then "forgives" him, keeps **north Pāñcāla (Ahicchatrā)** and returns the south (Kāmpilya, up to the Carmaṇvatī). Drupada, burning, longs for a son to kill Droṇa.

## G. LĀKṢĀGṚHA & THE FUGITIVE YEARS (1.129–1.157)
* **The plot (1.129–1.132):** Yudhiṣṭhira's popularity grows; citizens want him king. Duryodhana persuades his father to send the Pāṇḍavas to the festival town of **Vāraṇāvata**; the minister **Purocana** builds a mansion of hemp, resin, lac, ghee. `[NOT CE]` Kaṇika's Machiavellian lecture (App. I.81).
* **Vidura's riddle (1.133):** at the city boundary, Vidura speaks to Yudhiṣṭhira in cryptic speech (later called "mleccha speech", 1.135.6): *a weapon not of iron that cuts the body… the fire that burns dry grass does not burn creatures in burrows… the blind man knows no road… by the stars one finds direction* (1.133.19–23). Yudhiṣṭhira replies *"understood."* To Kuntī he decodes: *beware poison and fire; know every path* (1.133.28–29). They reach Vāraṇāvata on the **eighth day of Phālguna, under Rohiṇī** (1.133.30).
* **The tunnel (1.134–1.135):** Yudhiṣṭhira smells ghee and lac; a **miner** sent by Vidura proves himself with the "mleccha" password (1.135.6) and digs a tunnel with a flush trapdoor (1.135.16–17). They live there a year, hunting by day to learn the paths.
* **The fire (1.136):** Yudhiṣṭhira: **"Let us burn the armoury and Purocana, leave six living beings here, and flee unseen"** (1.136.4). Kuntī gives a night feast; **a Niṣāda woman with her five sons**, "driven by Time," drinks and sleeps there "like the dead" (1.136.7–8). In a howling wind **Bhīma sets fire to where Purocana sleeps** (1.136.9). Townspeople curse Dhṛtarāṣṭra and Duryodhana (1.136.11–13). Bhīma carries **Kuntī on his shoulder, the twins on his hips, Yudhiṣṭhira and Arjuna by the hands**, breaking trees (1.136.18–19). At dawn the townsfolk find the burnt house and Purocana's body (1.137.1–2); the Niṣāda bodies are taken for the Pāṇḍavas. In Hastinapura Dhṛtarāṣṭra offers funeral water, the Kauravas wail — **"Vidura grieved only a little, for he knew the truth"** (1.137.15–16). The Pāṇḍavas flee **south through the night, finding the way by the stars** — Vidura's riddle fulfilled (1.137.17–18); exhausted and thirsty, they beg Bhīma to carry them on (1.137.19–20). `[NOT CE]` Vidura's machine-driven boat on the Gaṅgā (App. I.81).
* **Hiḍimba & Hiḍimbā (1.139–1.144):** Bhīma kills the cannibal Hiḍimba; Yudhiṣṭhira forbids killing his sister Hiḍimbā and allows her union with Bhīma by day; **Ghaṭotkaca** is born, instantly grown, bald-headed (ghaṭa-utkaca), and promises to come whenever needed (1.143). Vyāsa meets them and guides them to **Ekacakrā** (1.144).
* **Baka (1.145–1.152):** living as brahmins, they learn a household must send its turn of food and a person to the rākṣasa Baka; Kuntī sends Bhīma; he eats Baka's food in front of him, wrestles him and **breaks his back over his knee** (1.151).
* **Draupadī's birth told (1.153–1.157):** a travelling brahmin tells how Drupada, through the priests Yāja and Upayāja, performed a sacrifice for a son: **Dhṛṣṭadyumna** rose from the fire armed, with a voice saying he was born **to kill Droṇa**; then from the **altar (vedi)** rose **Kṛṣṇā Draupadī** — dark, lotus-eyed, curly-haired, fragrant as a blue lotus for a krośa — and a bodiless voice: **"This best of women, Kṛṣṇā, will lead the Kṣatriyas to destruction"** (1.155.41–45).

## H. GANDHARVA, PURŌHITA, SWAYAMVARA, POLYANDRY (1.158–1.191)
* **Aṅgāraparṇa (1.158–1.173):** at night by the Gaṅgā the gandharva Aṅgāraparṇa (Citraratha) attacks; Arjuna burns his chariot with the **āgneya** weapon, spares him; friendship: gandharva horses and the **cākṣuṣī** vision; he tells the tales of Tapatī, Vasiṣṭha and Viśvāmitra. On his advice they take **Dhaumya** as priest (1.174).
* **The swayamvara (1.175–1.181):** Drupada has built a **stiff bow** and a **golden target with an aerial machine (yantra) with an aperture**; the winner must hit it with **five arrows** (1.176.10–11, 34). Kings, gods in vimānas, **Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma** attend; **Kṛṣṇa recognises the ash-veiled Pāṇḍavas "like fires covered with ash"** and points them out to Balarāma (1.178.8–10). King after king is thrown by the bow (1.178.15–17). As Arjuna rises from among the brahmins, they whisper: **"a bow not bent by world-famed kings headed by Karṇa and Śalya — how can this boy?"** (1.179.4). He circles the bow, bows, strings it **in the twinkling of an eye**, takes five arrows, brings down the target (1.179.14–16). Flowers fall on his head; Yudhiṣṭhira and the twins slip away home (1.179.17–21); **Draupadī, smiling, garlands him** (1.179.22). `[NOT CE]` Karṇa strings the bow and Draupadī says "I will not choose a sūta" (*1827).
* **The fight (1.180–1.181):** the kings attack. **Karṇa vs Arjuna** — three arrows stagger Karṇa; he asks: *"are you Dhanurveda, Rāma Bhārgava, Indra or Viṣṇu?"*; *"I am a brahmin"*; Karṇa withdraws before invincible brahma-power (1.181.9–21). **Bhīma lifts Śalya and throws him**, sparing him; the brahmins laugh (1.181.22–25). Kṛṣṇa calms the kings.
* **"Enjoy it together" (1.182):** at the potter's hut, Bhīma and Arjuna call "alms!"; Kuntī, **without looking**: *"Enjoy it all together"* — then, seeing the girl: *"What have I said!"* (1.182.2). **Kṛṣṇa visits them** that night (1.183). Dhṛṣṭadyumna spies on them and reports they are Kṣatriyas (1.184).
* **The debate & Vyāsa (1.185–1.191):** Drupada objects to five husbands; Yudhiṣṭhira cites precedent; **Vyāsa** privately reveals the **five Indras** and the sage's daughter who asked Śiva five times for a husband (1.189), gives Drupada divine sight to see them; the marriages take place on successive days (1.190–191).

## I. INDRAPRASTHA, EXILE, SUBHADRĀ (1.192–1.213)
* **Return & partition (1.192–1.199):** Duryodhana's schemes; Bhīṣma, Droṇa and Vidura argue for reconciliation; Vidura brings the Pāṇḍavas back; Dhṛtarāṣṭra gives them **half the kingdom — the wasteland Khāṇḍavaprastha**, where they build **Indraprastha** with Kṛṣṇa.
* **Nārada's rule (1.200–1.204):** to prevent quarrel over Draupadī, Nārada tells the tale of **Sunda and Upasunda** (brothers who killed each other over the apsaras Tilottamā); the rule: whoever intrudes on Draupadī with another brother goes into **twelve years' exile**.
* **Arjuna's exile (1.205–1.210):** to fetch weapons for a robbed brahmin he enters the armoury where Yudhiṣṭhira sits with Draupadī; he insists on exile. **Ulūpī** (Nāga princess) draws him into her father Kauravya's palace (1.206). `[NOT CE]` her water-invincibility boon (*2029). At coastal **Maṇalūra** he marries **Citrāṅgadā**, daughter of King Citravāhana, on condition her son continues her father's line; stays three years (1.207); frees five apsarases cursed as crocodiles (1.208–1.209); reaches Prabhāsa and Dvārakā, sports with Kṛṣṇa on Raivataka.
* **Subhadrā (1.211–1.213):** at the Raivataka festival Arjuna falls for **Subhadrā**, Kṛṣṇa's sister; Kṛṣṇa advises abduction, the Kṣatriya way. Arjuna, armoured, on **Kṛṣṇa's own golden chariot with the horses Sainya and Sugrīva**, seizes her after her mountain-worship and drives off (1.212.3–8). The Yādava assembly bell sounds war; Balarāma rages; **Kṛṣṇa calms them** — it's an honourable match. Births: **Abhimanyu** (Subhadrā) and **the five sons of Draupadī** — Prativindhya, Sutasoma, Śrutakarman, Śatānīka, Śrutasena (1.213).

## J. KHĀṆḌAVA-DAHANA (1.214–1.225)
On the Yamunā bank a huge, tawny, blazing brahmin approaches Kṛṣṇa and Arjuna: *"I am a great eater — give me one full meal."* — *"What food?"* — *"I am Agni. Give me the Khāṇḍava forest, which **Indra guards for his friend, the serpent Takṣaka**; whenever I blaze, he drowns me with rain"* (1.214–1.215.9). `[NOT CE]` the story of Agni's sickness from King Śvetaki's twelve-year ghee offerings. **Varuṇa** gives Arjuna **Gāṇḍīva** (formerly Soma's), **two inexhaustible quivers** and the **ape-bannered chariot** with **silver-white gandharva horses** (1.216.3–8); **Agni gives Kṛṣṇa the vajra-naved discus (cakra)** that returns to his hand (1.216.21–24); Varuṇa gives Kṛṣṇa the mace **Kaumodakī** (1.216.25). **On two chariots, one at each side of the forest**, they circle so fast they look like whirling wheels (1.217.1–3); creatures burn clinging to their young; lakes boil; Arjuna cuts down every escaping creature (1.217.5–12). Indra's storm is countered by Arjuna's arrow-canopy; the gods are defeated; a voice tells Indra to withdraw. **Aśvasena** (Takṣaka's son) escapes as Indra bewilders Arjuna (1.218.5–9). **Maya** the asura architect begs protection and is spared (1.219). Survivors: **Aśvasena, Maya and four Śārṅgaka fledglings** (1.219.40) — sons of the sage Mandapāla and the bird Jaritā, who praise Agni with Vedic hymns and are spared (1.220–1.225).

## K. SABHĀ PARVA (2.1–2.72)
* **The hall (2.1–2.4):** grateful Maya builds a peerless hall in **fourteen months** (2.3.34) — crystal floors, a lotus-pond of beryl leaves and jewelled stems that fools even kings (2.3.27–30); he brings Bhīma a great **mace** and Arjuna the conch **Devadatta** (2.3.18).
* **Nārada (2.5–2.11):** the great "kaccit" catechism of governance; descriptions of divine assemblies; Pāṇḍu's message from Indra's world urging the **Rājasūya**.
* **Jarāsaṁdha (2.12–2.22):** Kṛṣṇa says no Rājasūya while Jarāsaṁdha of Magadha holds **86 kings captive for sacrifice to Rudra**. His birth: two halves born to two queens, joined by the rākṣasī **Jarā** (2.16). Kṛṣṇa, Bhīma and Arjuna enter **Girivraja** disguised as snātaka brahmins, break the city's war-drums, challenge him. Bhīma and Jarāsaṁdha wrestle from the **first day of Kārttika for thirteen days and nights; on the fourteenth night** the king tires (2.21.17–18). Kṛṣṇa: **"Show us now in Jarāsaṁdha your divine power, the strength of the Wind!"** (2.22.4). Bhīma whirls him **a hundred times and breaks his back**, roaring so terribly that "pregnant women miscarried" (2.22.6–8). They free the kings and crown his son **Sahadeva**. `[NOT CE]` grass-blade signal; tearing the body in two.
* **Digvijaya (2.23–2.29):** Arjuna north, Bhīma east, Sahadeva south, Nakula west.
* **Rājasūya & Śiśupāla (2.30–2.42):** Bhīṣma names **Kṛṣṇa for the first honour**; Sahadeva offers it. **Śiśupāla** of Cedi — born with **three eyes and four arms, braying like a donkey** (2.40.1), whose death was foretold to come from the one in whose lap the extra limbs fell — abuses Bhīṣma, the Pāṇḍavas and Kṛṣṇa; Kṛṣṇa had promised his aunt to **forgive a hundred offences** (2.40.22). When Śiśupāla challenges him, **Kṛṣṇa severs his head with the discus**; a radiance rises from the body and enters Kṛṣṇa; rain from a cloudless sky, the earth shakes (2.42.21–25). Kṛṣṇa departs for **Dvārakā** (2.42.55–60).
* **The pool (2.43):** Duryodhana, staying on with Śakuni, mistakes crystal for water and lifts his cloth; later falls fully clothed into a real pool — **servants laugh; Bhīma, Arjuna and the twins laugh**; Yudhiṣṭhira sends him fresh clothes; he hits his forehead on a closed door that looked open (2.43.3–10). **Draupadī is not in this scene.** Burning with envy (amarṣa, 2.43.26) he returns.
* **The plot (2.44–2.52):** Śakuni: *don't fight them — I'll win it all at dice* (2.44–2.45). To his father Duryodhana adds what never happened: **"Kṛṣṇa laughed at me with Pārtha, and Draupadī with the women"** (2.46.30) — though even he admits **the twins held him up and Sahadeva showed him the way** (2.46.33–34). Dhṛtarāṣṭra builds a gaming hall, overrules Vidura, sends Vidura to invite Yudhiṣṭhira (2.47–2.52). Yudhiṣṭhira: *"Dice is the root of ruin… but challenged, I never turn back — that is my vow"* (2.52.16).
* **The game (2.53–2.58):** Śakuni throws for Duryodhana; every throw: **"Jitam!"** Yudhiṣṭhira loses jewels, gold, his war-chariot, elephants, slaves, army, treasury, kingdom; then **Nakula, Sahadeva, Arjuna, Bhīma, himself**; Śakuni: *you still have one stake — Kṛṣṇā Pāñcālī; win yourself back* (2.58.31); **Yudhiṣṭhira stakes Draupadī** (2.58.37) — "Jitam!" `[note]` the CE never names the dice's material.
* **Draupadī in the hall (2.59–2.65):** Vidura protests (2.59). **Prātikāmin** summons her; she sends back **the question: "Whom did you lose first — yourself, or me?"** (2.60.7). Duḥśāsana drags her **by the hair once sprinkled with the Rājasūya's holy water** (2.60.22–24), she in her **period, in a single garment** (2.60.25), mocking: **"Cry to Kṛṣṇa, Jiṣṇu, Hari and Nara for help!"** (2.60.26). She addresses the elders (2.60.29–34); **Karṇa laughs aloud, Śakuni applauds** (2.60.38); **Bhīṣma: "dharma is subtle… I cannot decide"** (2.60.40–42). Bhīma threatens to burn Yudhiṣṭhira's hands; Arjuna restrains him (2.61.1–10). **Vikarṇa** alone of the Kauravas rules her unwon (2.61.11–24). **Karṇa: "one husband is ordained; she is a bandhakī… strip the Pāṇḍavas and Draupadī"** (2.61.35–38). The Pāṇḍavas drop their upper cloths. **Duḥśāsana pulls her garment — and another identical garment appears, again and again** (2.61.40–41); a roar of awe (2.61.42); **Bhīma's oath to drink Duḥśāsana's blood** (2.61.43–46); a mountain of cloth; **Duḥśāsana sits down exhausted and ashamed** (2.61.48). `[NOT CE]` Draupadī's prayer to Kṛṣṇa (*542–*550), "Dharma covered her with many-coloured cloths" (*553), "108 garments" (*552). Vidura's Prahlāda-Sudhanvan exemplum (2.61). **Duryodhana bares his left thigh, smiling at Karṇa** (2.63.10–12); **Bhīma's thigh-oath** — fire seems to blaze from every pore (2.63.14–15). **A jackal howls in Dhṛtarāṣṭra's fire-sanctuary**, asses answer (2.63.22); Gāndhārī and Vidura warn the king; Dhṛtarāṣṭra rebukes Duryodhana and offers Draupadī boons: **(1) Yudhiṣṭhira free — so Prativindhya is never called a slave's son; (2) the four brothers free with chariots and bows; (3) refused — "greed destroys dharma; a Kṣatriya woman may take two"** (2.63.27–36). Dhṛtarāṣṭra returns everything.
* **The second game (2.66–2.67):** Duryodhana, Duḥśāsana, Karṇa and Śakuni panic the old king into recalling them for **one throw: losers go to the forest for twelve years in deerskins and live the thirteenth unrecognised — if found, twelve more** (2.66.18–19). Yudhiṣṭhira, knowing it is ruin, returns (2.67.4). **"Jitam!"** (2.67.21).
* **The four vows (2.68):** in deerskins, mocked by Duḥśāsana: **Bhīma** — drink Duḥśāsana's blood, kill Duryodhana with the mace and put his foot on his head (2.68.21–29); **Arjuna** — the earth will drink the blood of Duryodhana, Karṇa, Śakuni and Duḥśāsana; Himālaya may move before my word (2.68.30–35); **Sahadeva** — "these are not dice, fool, they are arrows you have chosen" — kill Śakuni (2.68.38–41); **Nakula** — clear the earth of Dhṛtarāṣṭra's sons (2.68.43–45).
* **Departure (2.69–2.71):** Kuntī stays with Vidura. Vidura describes the departure to the blind king: **Yudhiṣṭhira covers his face** (lest his angry eyes burn the people), **Bhīma spreads his arms**, **Arjuna scatters sand** (so will his arrows fall), **Sahadeva smears his face**, **Nakula smears his body with dust**, **Draupadī covers her face with her hair, weeping**, **Dhaumya walks ahead singing the Sāmans of Yama** — *"so will the Kuru priests sing when the Bhāratas lie slain"* (2.71.3–22). **Draupadī: "In the fourteenth year, the wives of those who did this — husbands, sons and kin slain, smeared with their blood, hair loose — will make the water-offerings and enter Hastinapura"** (2.71.18–20). Omens: lightning from a clear sky, earthquake, **an untimely eclipse**, a meteor (2.71.25–28). **Nārada, in the hall: "In the fourteenth year the Kauravas will perish"** (2.71.29–30).
* **Night (2.72):** Dhṛtarāṣṭra to Sañjaya: *"When the gods want a man's ruin they first take his reason… Time does not raise a stick to cut a head; Time's power is only this — to make one see things reversed"* (2.72.8–11). He remembers **"Pāñcālī in a single garment, gazing at the Pāṇḍavas… her pitiful eyes could burn the earth"** (2.72.15–18).
