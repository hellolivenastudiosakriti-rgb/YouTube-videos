## A. BHĪṢMA PARVA — DAYS 1–10 (6.1–6.117)

### A1. Before the first arrow (6.1–6.22)
* **Rules of war (6.1.27–32):** after the day's fighting, friendship as before; word answered by word; one who leaves the ranks must not be killed; chariot fights chariot, elephant elephant, horse horse, foot-soldier foot-soldier; warn before striking; never strike the trusting, the terrified, one engaged with another, the careless, the fleeing, one whose weapons are gone or armour off; **never charioteers, draught animals, weapon-carriers, drummers or conch-blowers**.
* **Vyāsa & Sañjaya (6.2):** Vyāsa offers Dhṛtarāṣṭra sight to watch the war; he refuses — *I don't want to see my kin killed* — so Vyāsa gives **Sañjaya divine sight**: he will see everything, day or night, openly or in thought, and no weapon will harm him.
* **Structure shock (6.14):** the parva opens with **Sañjaya returning on the tenth day: "Bhīṣma is slain… he lies on a bed of arrows"** — then the story rewinds to Day 1 in his telling. *(Ideal Part III cold open.)*
* Geography of the world (Jambūkhaṇḍa, Bhūmi — 6.5–6.13); the arrays (6.15–6.22).

### A2. The Gītā (6.23–6.40)
* **The conches (6.23.12–19):** Bhīṣma roars like a lion and blows his conch first; drums, cymbals, cow-horns erupt; **Kṛṣṇa and Arjuna in the great chariot yoked with white horses** blow **Pāñcajanya** and **Devadatta**; Bhīma **Pauṇḍra**; Yudhiṣṭhira **Anantavijaya**; Nakula **Sughoṣa**; Sahadeva **Maṇipuṣpaka**; then Kāśya, Śikhaṇḍin, Dhṛṣṭadyumna, Virāṭa, Sātyaki, Drupada, the five Draupadeyas and Abhimanyu, each his own — **"the uproar tore the hearts of Dhṛtarāṣṭra's sons, echoing through sky and earth"** (6.23.19).
* Arjuna asks Kṛṣṇa to halt between the armies; seeing teachers, grandfathers, uncles, brothers, sons and friends, **his limbs fail, Gāṇḍīva slips from his hand** (6.23.28–29); he sits down on the chariot (6.23.47).
* Eighteen chapters of the **Bhagavad Gītā** (= 6.23–6.40); in chapter 11 (= 6.33) Kṛṣṇa grants Arjuna divine sight and shows the **devouring cosmic form**, warriors rushing into its fanged mouths "like moths into fire." Arjuna: *"my delusion is destroyed."* `[NOT CE]` Arjuna's hymn to Durgā before the war; time "freezing" on the battlefield.
* **Yudhiṣṭhira's walk (6.41):** he removes his armour and walks barefoot across to the Kaurava ranks; the army jeers; he asks **Bhīṣma, Droṇa, Kṛpa and Śalya** for leave and blessings; each says *"I am bound to the Kauravas by wealth (arthasya puruṣo dāsaḥ) — but victory is yours."* **Yuyutsu** crosses over to the Pāṇḍava side.

### A4. The bed of arrows (6.115–6.117)
Both armies stop. Bhīṣma's head hangs; the kings bring soft pillows — he refuses; **Arjuna shoots three arrows into the ground to pillow his head** (6.115). Thirsty, he refuses palace water; **Arjuna pierces the earth and a stream of pure water (Gaṅgā) rises to his mouth** (6.116). Bhīṣma pleads with Duryodhana for peace — refused (6.116). At night **Karṇa** comes, weeping: *"I am Rādhā's son whom you always hated."* Bhīṣma: **"You are Kaunteya, not Rādheya — I learned it from Nārada"** (6.117.9); *I spoke harshly only to dampen your pride; now go, fight* — Karṇa takes leave to fight.

## B. DROṆA PARVA — DAYS 11–15 (7.1–7.173)
* **Day 11:** Droṇa made commander (7.5); **vows to capture Yudhiṣṭhira alive** — if Arjuna is drawn away (7.11–7.12).
* **Day 12:** the **Saṁśaptakas** (Trigarta suicide-squad) swear to kill Arjuna or die and lure him off (7.16–7.17). Old **Bhagadatta of Prāgjyotiṣa** on the elephant **Supratīka** routs the Pāṇḍavas; he hurls the **Vaiṣṇava weapon at Arjuna — Kṛṣṇa takes it on his chest, where it becomes the Vaijayantī garland**; Arjuna kills elephant and king (7.25–7.28).
* **Day 13 — Abhimanyu (7.33–7.51):** Droṇa forms the **cakravyūha**. Yudhiṣṭhira asks Abhimanyu: only Arjuna, Kṛṣṇa, Pradyumna and you can break it. Abhimanyu: **"My father taught me how to break in — I cannot get out if trouble comes"** (7.34.19). He breaks in; **Jayadratha, with Śiva's boon, seals the breach**, holding back all the other Pāṇḍavas (7.41). Abhimanyu kills Duryodhana's son **Lakṣmaṇa** and many (7.45); **six great warriors** surround him (7.46); on Droṇa's advice **Karṇa cuts his bow from behind**, his horses and charioteers are killed; armourless, bowless, he fights with sword and shield, then a **chariot wheel**, then a mace; **Duḥśāsana's son** and he both fall; the son rises first and **strikes Abhimanyu's head with the mace** (7.47–7.48). Evening: Arjuna returns, learns, and **vows to kill Jayadratha before sunset tomorrow — or enter fire** (7.51). Night: in a dream Kṛṣṇa takes Arjuna to Śiva, who renews the Pāśupata (7.57). `[NOT CE]` Abhimanyu learning the vyūha in the womb.
* **Day 14 — Jayadratha (7.63–7.121):** Droṇa places Jayadratha deep behind a huge formation. Arjuna cuts through; horses tire — Arjuna makes a lake with arrows, Kṛṣṇa unyokes and tends them on the battlefield. **Sātyaki vs Bhūriśravas:** about to be killed, Sātyaki is saved as **Arjuna cuts off Bhūriśravas's arm**; Bhūriśravas sits in yoga; **Sātyaki beheads him** — condemned by all (7.117–7.118). Late in the day, **"as the sun was swiftly going", Kṛṣṇa: "Cut off his head now — the sun is about to set"** (7.121.15–16) and tells the secret: Jayadratha's father **Vṛddhakṣatra** decreed that whoever made his son's head fall to the ground would have his own head burst — Arjuna's arrows carry **the head into Vṛddhakṣatra's lap** where he sits in prayer; he rises, it falls, **his head splits in a hundred pieces** (7.121). `[NOT CE]` Kṛṣṇa covering the sun with darkness (App. I.16).
* **Night 14 (7.129–7.154):** the fighting continues by **torchlight** — a rule broken. **Ghaṭotkaca** in his giant form, with rākṣasa illusions, routs the Kauravas; terrified, they beg Karṇa: **Karṇa hurls Indra's Śakti, saved for Arjuna**; Ghaṭotkaca swells to an enormous size as he falls, crushing an akṣauhiṇī (7.154). The Pāṇḍavas weep — **Kṛṣṇa dances and roars with joy**: *now Karṇa is killable* (7.155); he explains he removed Jarāsaṁdha, Śiśupāla and Ekalavya for Arjuna's sake (7.156).
* **Day 15 — Droṇa (7.157–7.173):** Droṇa kills **Drupada and Virāṭa** (7.161). Kṛṣṇa: *he cannot be beaten while he holds a weapon — tell him Aśvatthāman is dead.* Bhīma kills an elephant named **Aśvatthāman** and shouts it; Droṇa asks Yudhiṣṭhira, the truthful; **prompted by Kṛṣṇa, sunk in fear of falsehood yet clinging to victory, Yudhiṣṭhira says "Aśvatthāman is slain" — and indistinctly, "the elephant"**; his chariot, which always ran **four fingers above the ground**, now **touches the earth** (7.164.104–107). Droṇa lays down his weapons and sits in yoga; **Dhṛṣṭadyumna beheads him** despite Arjuna's cries (7.165). Aśvatthāman, wild with grief, releases the **Nārāyaṇa weapon**; Kṛṣṇa orders everyone to **drop weapons and get down from their chariots**; only **Bhīma** refuses and is engulfed — Kṛṣṇa and Arjuna drag him down (7.166–7.171). Arjuna and Sātyaki quarrel with Dhṛṣṭadyumna over Droṇa's killing.

## C. KARṆA PARVA — DAYS 16–17 (8.1–8.69)
* Karṇa made commander (8.6). **Śalya** insulted at being asked to be Karṇa's charioteer; Duryodhana flatters him with the tale of **Śiva destroying the Three Cities** (8.24), Brahmā as Śiva's charioteer. Śalya agrees on condition he may say what he likes — and **demoralises Karṇa all the way** (the crow-and-swan fable, 8.27–8.30). Karṇa recalls his curses: **Paraśurāma's** (he lied that he was a brahmin; a worm bored his thigh while the guru slept on his lap — "your weapon will fail you when you need it most") and **the brahmin whose cow he killed** ("your wheel will sink in battle") (8.29).
* **The quarrel (8.46–8.49):** wounded, humiliated by Karṇa, Yudhiṣṭhira insults Arjuna: *give your Gāṇḍīva to someone else!* Arjuna, bound by a secret vow to kill anyone who says that, **draws his sword on his brother**; Kṛṣṇa stops him with the parables of the **hunter Balāka** and the **truthful ascetic Kauśika**, and teaches that **insulting an elder with "tvam" (you) is equal to killing him**; Arjuna does so, then turns the sword on himself; Kṛṣṇa: *praise yourself — self-praise is suicide for the honourable* (8.49).
* **Duḥśāsana (8.61):** Bhīma smashes his chariot, **plants a foot on his throat, cuts open his chest and drinks his warm blood**, tasting it: *"sweeter than mother's milk, honey and ghee, wine, water, curds"*; warriors drop their weapons and flee crying "he is not human" (8.61.6–10). Arjuna kills Karṇa's son **Vṛṣasena**.
* **The final duel (8.63–8.67):** gods and demons divide their support. **Karṇa's serpent-arrow — Aśvasena** (the survivor of Khāṇḍava) — flies at Arjuna's head; **Kṛṣṇa presses the chariot into the earth**; it strikes off only **Arjuna's diadem**; Arjuna kills the serpent (8.66). **Karṇa's chariot-wheel sinks** (the brahmin's curse), his weapon-knowledge fails (Paraśurāma's curse). Karṇa: *"Wait — dharma says do not strike a man whose wheel is stuck!"* **Kṛṣṇa: "Now you remember dharma? Where was your dharma when Draupadī was dragged into the hall in one garment… when Yudhiṣṭhira was cheated at dice… when the boy Abhimanyu was surrounded?"** (8.67.1–5). Karṇa keeps fighting — **brahmāstra** against Arjuna's, **Vāruṇa** against Arjuna's fire (8.67.8–11). **Arjuna's añjalika arrow takes Karṇa's head** as the sun sets (8.67). His radiance enters the sun. `[NOT CE]` Bhūdevī's curse, Karṇa giving his gold tooth to a disguised Kṛṣṇa.

## D. ŚALYA PARVA — DAY 18 (9.1–9.64)
* **Śalya**, commander, is killed by **Yudhiṣṭhira's spear** (9.16). **Sahadeva kills Śakuni** (and Ulūka) (9.27). Of eleven akṣauhiṇīs only Duryodhana, Kṛpa, Kṛtavarman and Aśvatthāman remain.
* **The lake (9.28–9.32):** Duryodhana hides inside **Dvaipāyana lake**, solidifying the waters by māyā; the Pāṇḍavas taunt him out; Yudhiṣṭhira foolishly offers: *fight any one of us with any weapon — win, and the kingdom is yours.* He chooses the **mace** and Bhīma. **Balarāma** arrives from his Sarasvatī pilgrimage (the tīrtha-yātrā, 9.34–9.53) to watch his two pupils.
* **The mace duel (9.54–9.58):** Kṛṣṇa warns Arjuna: *Bhīma is stronger, Duryodhana more skilled — in fair fight he cannot win; let Bhīma keep his vow by trickery* (9.57.3–17). **Arjuna slaps his own thigh, Bhīma watching** (9.57.18); Bhīma **smashes Duryodhana's thighs** and kicks his head (9.57–9.58).
* **Balarāma's rage (9.59):** he lifts his plough to kill Bhīma; Kṛṣṇa restrains him; Balarāma leaves calling Bhīma a cheat.
* **Duryodhana's last words (9.60):** *"I ruled, I enjoyed, I gave, my enemies were under my feet — who has had a better end?"*; **flowers rain on him, gandharvas play**; the Pāṇḍavas are ashamed; Kṛṣṇa: *these deaths could not have come by fair means — the gods too used such ways* (9.60).
* **The burning chariot (9.61):** at the Kaurava camp Kṛṣṇa makes Arjuna get down first, then descends; **the ape vanishes from the banner and the chariot bursts into flame and falls to ash** — burnt inside long ago by Droṇa's and Karṇa's weapons, held together only by Kṛṣṇa (9.61.12–14). Kṛṣṇa goes to Hastinapura to calm Gāndhārī (9.62). Dying, Duryodhana **anoints Aśvatthāman commander** (9.64).

## E. SAUPTIKA PARVA — THE NIGHT (10.1–10.18)
* Under a banyan, Aśvatthāman watches **an owl kill sleeping crows one by one** (10.1) — the idea is born. Kṛpa argues; Aśvatthāman goes. At the camp gate a **colossal being** blocks him, swallowing his weapons; he offers himself as oblation to **Śiva**, who gives him a sword and **enters his body** (10.6–10.7).
* **The massacre (10.8):** he kicks awake **Dhṛṣṭadyumna** and **strangles/tramples him to death like an animal**; kills **Śikhaṇḍin**, **the five sons of Draupadī**, Uttamaujas, Yudhāmanyu and thousands — Kṛpa and Kṛtavarman set fire to the camp and kill those who flee. The Pāṇḍavas, Kṛṣṇa and Sātyaki are away. Duryodhana hears the news at dawn and **dies content** (10.9).
* **Draupadī's demand (10.11):** she sits fasting: *bring me the jewel on Aśvatthāman's head.* Bhīma pursues; Kṛṣṇa and Arjuna follow.
* **Brahmaśiras (10.13–10.15):** cornered at the Gaṅgā beside Vyāsa, Aśvatthāman releases the **brahmaśiras** "for the destruction of the Pāṇḍavas"; Arjuna counters; Nārada and Vyāsa stand between the fires; **Arjuna withdraws his** — Aśvatthāman cannot, and turns it on **the wombs of the Pāṇḍava women**.
* **The curse (10.16):** Kṛṣṇa: *the child in Uttarā's womb will live — I will revive him; he will rule sixty years* (10.16.14). **"For three thousand years you will wander this earth, alone, friendless, stinking of pus and blood"** (10.16.10). He surrenders his head-jewel; **Draupadī gives it to Yudhiṣṭhira to wear**.

## F. STRĪ PARVA — THE WOMEN (11.1–11.27)
* Dhṛtarāṣṭra embraces the Pāṇḍavas; warned by Kṛṣṇa, they push forward an **iron statue of Bhīma** which the blind king **crushes to powder**, then weeps (11.12).
* **Gāndhārī**, about to curse, is calmed by Vyāsa; her glance through the blindfold falls on **Yudhiṣṭhira's toe-nails and blackens them** (11.15).
* With **divine sight given by Vyāsa** she sees the whole battlefield — widows searching for heads, jackals, vultures; she laments every hero (11.16–11.25), then **curses Kṛṣṇa: "in the thirty-sixth year your own kin will destroy each other and you will die alone in the forest"**; Kṛṣṇa smiles: *I knew; the Vṛṣṇis can be destroyed only by themselves* (11.25).
* **The toll (11.26.9–10):** **1,660,020,000 slain; 24,165 unaccounted.** The great cremations.
* **Kuntī's secret (11.27):** at the water-offerings Kuntī says: *offer water to Karṇa too — he was your eldest brother.* Yudhiṣṭhira breaks.

## G. ŚĀNTI & ANUŚĀSANA — THE BED OF ARROWS (12.1–13.154)
* Yudhiṣṭhira, broken, wants to renounce; brothers and Draupadī argue; **Nārada tells Karṇa's story and curses** (12.1–12.5); Yudhiṣṭhira **curses all women never to keep a secret** (12.6.10). Coronation; the rākṣasa **Cārvāka** disguised as a brahmin abuses him and is **burned by the brahmins' humkāra** (12.39). Kṛṣṇa takes him to Bhīṣma (12.46ff.).
* **Bhīṣma's teachings:** Rājadharma (12.1–12.128), Āpaddharma (12.129–12.167, incl. Viśvāmitra stealing dog-meat in famine, 12.139), Mokṣadharma (12.168–12.353, incl. Śuka, the Nārāyaṇīya); Anuśāsana — effort vs destiny (13.6), Śiva's thousand names (13.17), "ahiṁsā paramo dharmaḥ" (13.113–13.117), **Viṣṇu's thousand names (13.135)**.
* **Death (13.153–13.154):** *"fifty-eight nights I have lain on these sharp arrows; they felt like a hundred years"* (13.153.27); *"this is the holy month of Māgha, the bright fortnight"* (13.153.28). He blesses all, holds his breath in yoga; his life bursts through his crown; flowers fall; **Gaṅgā rises from the water weeping for her son**; Kṛṣṇa consoles her (13.154).

## H. ĀŚVAMEDHIKA (14.1–14.96)
Vyāsa prescribes a horse sacrifice; **King Marutta's buried Himālayan gold** is brought (14.3–14.10, 14.62). Arjuna admits he has forgotten the Gītā; Kṛṣṇa recites the **Anugītā** (14.16–14.50). Kṛṣṇa meets the sage **Uttaṅka** on his way home. **Uttarā bears a still-born son; Kṛṣṇa revives him — Parīkṣit** (14.65–14.69). Arjuna follows the horse across the land: Trigartas, **Vajradatta** (Bhagadatta's son), the Saindhavas (**Duḥśalā** begs mercy for her grandson); at **Maṇalūra** his son **Babhruvāhana**, goaded by **Ulūpī**, **kills Arjuna**; Ulūpī's serpent-jewel revives him (14.78–14.82). At the end, **a mongoose, half its body golden**, rolls in the sacrificial ground: *this great sacrifice is not worth a handful of barley-flour given by a starving brahmin family to a guest* (14.92–14.96).

## I. ĀŚRAMAVĀSIKA (15.1–15.47)
**Fifteen years** of honoured life (15.4.12); Bhīma's private taunts wound Dhṛtarāṣṭra; he, **Gāndhārī, Kuntī, Vidura and Sañjaya** retire to the forest. **Vidura**, emaciated, meets Yudhiṣṭhira under a tree and **enters his body by yoga** (15.33). Vyāsa, for one night, calls the dead warriors out of the **Gaṅgā** — **Karṇa, Abhimanyu, Duryodhana, Ghaṭotkaca, Droṇa** — free of all enmity; the widows who wish may follow their husbands into the river (15.39–15.41). Later **Dhṛtarāṣṭra, Gāndhārī and Kuntī die seated in meditation in a forest fire**; Sañjaya leaves for the Himālaya (15.45).

## J. MAUSALA (16.1–16.9)
**Thirty-sixth year.** Young Yādavas dress **Sāmba as a pregnant woman** and mock the sages Viśvāmitra, Kaṇva and Nārada: *what will she bear?* — **"an iron pestle that will destroy the Vṛṣṇis and Andhakas"**; next day Sāmba bears it (16.2). It is ground to powder and thrown into the sea; it grows as **eraka** reeds. At **Prabhāsa**, drunk, **Sātyaki mocks Kṛtavarman for the night massacre**, Kṛtavarman mocks Sātyaki for killing armless Bhūriśravas; **Sātyaki beheads Kṛtavarman**; the eraka reeds become iron clubs in their hands and **all the Yādavas kill each other** (16.4). Balarāma, in yoga, leaves his body — **a great white serpent goes into the sea**. Kṛṣṇa lies in the forest in yoga; **the hunter Jarā mistakes him for a deer and pierces the sole of his foot**, falls at his feet; Kṛṣṇa consoles him and departs (16.5). Arjuna arrives, cremates Vasudeva and the dead, leads the women away; **the sea swallows Dvārakā** (16.8). In Pañcanada, **cowherd robbers (Ābhīras) attack; Arjuna can barely string Gāṇḍīva; his astras will not come; the inexhaustible quivers empty** (16.8). Vyāsa: *Time gave you everything; Time takes it back* (16.9).

## K. MAHĀPRASTHĀNIKA (17.1–17.3)
**Parīkṣit** crowned in Hastinapura, **Vajra** (Kṛṣṇa's great-grandson) in Indraprastha (17.1.8–9). The Pāṇḍavas and Draupadī in bark garments walk east, then south, then west to sunken Dvārakā; **Agni appears: return Gāṇḍīva and the quivers to Varuṇa** — Arjuna throws them into the sea (17.1). A **dog** follows. Climbing beyond the Himālaya one by one they fall: **Draupadī** (partial to Arjuna), **Sahadeva** (pride in wisdom), **Nakula** (pride in beauty), **Arjuna** (boasted he'd kill all enemies in a day; scorned other archers), **Bhīma** (gluttony, boasting of strength) (17.2). **Indra** arrives in his chariot; Yudhiṣṭhira refuses to abandon the devoted dog; **the dog becomes Dharma** (17.3).

## L. SVARGĀROHAṆA (18.1–18.5)
In heaven Yudhiṣṭhira sees **Duryodhana enthroned** and refuses to stay without his brothers (18.1). A messenger leads him down a reeking path of corpses, blades and boiling rivers; voices cry *"stay — your presence comforts us"* — **Karṇa, Bhīma, Arjuna, the twins, Draupadī**; Yudhiṣṭhira chooses to stay in hell with them (18.2). The illusion vanishes — **it was the price of the half-truth to Droṇa** (18.3); he bathes in the heavenly Gaṅgā, sheds his human body and sees all in their divine forms — Kṛṣṇa as Viṣṇu, Karṇa with Sūrya, Bhīṣma among the Vasus (18.4–18.5).
**The frame closes (18.5.26–29):** the story ends; the priests complete the sacrifice; **Āstīka rejoices at having freed the serpents**; Janamejaya dismisses the brahmins and returns **from Takṣaśilā to Hastinapura**. **Vyāsa's cry (18.5.49):** *"With arms raised I cry out, yet no one hears me: from dharma come wealth and desire — why is dharma not followed?"*
