## A. ĀRAṆYAKA PARVA — THE TWELVE YEARS (3.1–3.299)

### A1. Leaving Hastinapura (3.1–3.10)
Citizens follow the exiles; Yudhiṣṭhira sends them back. Brahmins insist on coming; how to feed them? **Dhaumya teaches Yudhiṣṭhira the 108 names of Sūrya** (3.3); standing in the water, Yudhiṣṭhira prays; **Sūrya appears, blazing like fire: "I will give you food for twelve years — fruit, roots, meat and greens cooked in your kitchen will be inexhaustible"** (3.4.1–3); they cook in their kitchen and feed all (3.4.5). `[NOT CE]` the **copper vessel (tāmra piṭhara / "akṣaya-pātra")** that gives food until Draupadī has eaten — star passage 3*21.
* **Vidura exiled & recalled (3.5–3.7):** Vidura again tells Dhṛtarāṣṭra to return the kingdom; the king says "go wherever you like"; Vidura goes to the Pāṇḍavas in the Kāmyaka forest; the king, remorseful, faints, sends Sañjaya to bring him back.
* **Maitreya's curse (3.10–3.11):** the sage Maitreya urges Duryodhana to make peace; Duryodhana **slaps his elephant-trunk-like thigh** in contempt (3.11.28); Maitreya curses: *in the war Bhīma will break that thigh*.
* **Kirmīra (3.11–3.12):** Baka's brother blocks their path in the Kāmyaka forest; Bhīma kills him.

### A2. Kṛṣṇa in the forest & Draupadī's fire (3.13–3.36)
* Kṛṣṇa, the Bhojas and Vṛṣṇis visit (3.13). **Draupadī weeps before Kṛṣṇa** — the hair-dragging, the single garment, her husbands' silence; Kṛṣṇa promises that **the wives of those who angered her will weep** and that the Pāṇḍavas will rule again. Kṛṣṇa explains he was away fighting **Śālva of Saubha** (the flying city) during the dice game (3.14–3.23).
* **Dvaitavana & the great debate (3.24–3.36):** Draupadī, Bhīma vs Yudhiṣṭhira — *anger vs forgiveness, human effort vs fate*. Draupadī: the creator plays with beings like puppets; Yudhiṣṭhira: *I practise dharma not for its fruit*. Bhīma: *let us fight now, thirteen months are like thirteen years*. Yudhiṣṭhira holds to the pledge.
* **Vyāsa's instruction (3.37):** Vyāsa gives Yudhiṣṭhira the **Pratismṛti** knowledge to pass to Arjuna: go to Indra and Śiva for divine weapons — Bhīṣma, Droṇa, Kṛpa, Karṇa and Aśvatthāman all hold great astras.

### A3. Arjuna's quest (3.37–3.46, 3.161–3.172)
* **Austerity & the Kirāta (3.38–3.41):** alone in the Himālaya, Arjuna fasts on fruit, then dry leaves, then air. A boar (the dānava Mūka) charges; Arjuna and a **hunter (Kirāta) — Śiva in disguise, with Umā and his host** — shoot it at the same instant. They fight: arrows, then bare fists; **the inexhaustible quivers run empty**, Gāṇḍīva is snatched away, Arjuna is crushed senseless (3.39). Recognising Śiva, he worships; Śiva grants the **Pāśupata**: to be released by mind, eye, word or bow — **never against a weak foe** (3.40–3.41). The **Lokapālas** (Yama, Varuṇa, Kubera, Indra) give their weapons (3.41).
* **Indraloka (3.42–3.44):** Mātali brings Indra's chariot; Arjuna lives five years in Amarāvatī, learns weapons and **song, dance and music from the gandharva Citrasena**; sees the apsarases dance (3.44.29 names Urvaśī among them). `[NOT CE]` **Urvaśī's curse of eunuchhood** (App. I.6) — in the CE Bṛhannaḍā is Arjuna's own choice (4.2.21).
* **Guru-dakṣiṇā to Indra (3.165–3.172, told on return):** Arjuna alone destroys the **Nivātakavacas** under the sea and the **Kālakeyas/Paulomas** of the flying city Hiraṇyapura.

### A4. The pilgrimage years (3.47–3.160)
* **Nala & Damayantī (3.50–3.78)** — told by the sage Bṛhadaśva to the grieving Yudhiṣṭhira: a king who lost everything at dice and won it back (film use: a 90-second shadow-play inlay).
* **Tīrthayātrā with Lomaśa (3.80–3.153):** sacred fords and their stories — Agastya, Ṛṣyaśṛṅga, Paraśurāma, Cyavana & Sukanyā, Māndhātṛ, Śibi, Aṣṭāvakra, Yavakrīta (inlays).
* **Gandhamādana (3.140–3.153):** Draupadī collapses in a mountain storm; **Ghaṭotkaca**, remembered by Bhīma, carries her and the others (3.144–3.145). A thousand-petalled **saugandhika** lotus drifts to Draupadī; Bhīma goes to find it. On the way **an old monkey lies across the path — Hanūmān**, Bhīma's elder brother (both sons of Vāyu); Bhīma **cannot lift his tail**; Hanūmān shows the form that leapt the ocean and promises: *I will sit on Arjuna's banner and roar to terrify your enemies* (3.146–3.150). Bhīma fights Kubera's yakṣas at the lotus-lake (3.151–3.152).
* **Jaṭāsura (3.154):** a rākṣasa disguised as a brahmin abducts Yudhiṣṭhira, the twins and Draupadī; Bhīma kills him.
* **Arjuna returns (3.155–3.164)** after five years with the weapons; **Bhīma kills Maṇimat** (Kubera's friend) (3.157–3.158).

### A5. The python (3.173–3.178)
A giant python seizes Bhīma — the ancient king **Nahuṣa**, cursed by Agastya; he will free his prey only if someone answers his questions. Yudhiṣṭhira answers: *a brahmin is known by truth, charity, forgiveness, conduct, austerity — not by birth* (3.177). Nahuṣa is released and ascends.

### A6. Mārkaṇḍeya & Satyabhāmā (3.179–3.224)
Kṛṣṇa and Satyabhāmā visit; the ageless sage **Mārkaṇḍeya** tells of the ages, the **Fish (Matsya) and the flood** (3.185), the child on the banyan leaf, the warrior Kārttikeya. **Draupadī & Satyabhāmā (3.222–3.224):** Satyabhāmā asks how Draupadī keeps five husbands devoted — *by service, alertness, never by spells or drugs.*

### A7. Ghoṣayātrā — Duryodhana's humiliation (3.225–3.243)
On Karṇa's and Śakuni's urging, Duryodhana goes to Dvaitavana under the pretext of inspecting cattle (ghoṣa) to flaunt his wealth before the exiles. The gandharva king **Citrasena** forbids them; Karṇa is beaten and flees; **Duryodhana and the Kaurava women are captured**. Bhīma laughs; **Yudhiṣṭhira commands his brothers to free them** — "against outsiders we are a hundred and five." **Arjuna** defeats Citrasena (his own music-teacher from Indraloka) and frees Duryodhana (3.234–3.235). Shamed, **Duryodhana sits down to fast to death (prāya)** (3.238); the Dānavas carry him in a vision to the netherworld and tell him he was born of Śiva's grace and they will possess Bhīṣma, Droṇa and Karṇa in the war (3.239–3.240); Karṇa vows to kill Arjuna and makes a world-conquest. `[NOT CE]` Karṇa's detailed digvijaya (App. I.24). Duryodhana performs the **Vaiṣṇava** sacrifice (with a golden plough) since the Rājasūya is denied him (3.241–3.243).

### A8. Mudgala, Durvāsas, Jayadratha (3.244–3.256)
* **Mudgala (3.246):** the sage who lives on gleaned grain; **Durvāsas** tests him by eating his food six times; Mudgala stays serene and is offered heaven. `[NOT CE]` Durvāsas with 10,000 disciples at the Pāṇḍava hermitage and Kṛṣṇa eating one grain of rice (App. I.25).
* **Jayadratha (3.248–3.256):** while the brothers hunt, King **Jayadratha of Sindhu** (Duḥśalā's husband) sees Draupadī alone, proposes, and **abducts her by force**; Dhaumya follows the chariot crying out. The Pāṇḍavas give chase and smash his army; Draupadī points out each warrior from the chariot. Bhīma catches the fleeing Jayadratha, beats him, **shaves his head leaving five tufts** and makes him declare himself the Pāṇḍavas' slave; Yudhiṣṭhira frees him. Burning with shame, Jayadratha does austerity: **Śiva grants he will hold back all the Pāṇḍavas except Arjuna for one day** (3.256).

### A9. Rāmopākhyāna & Sāvitrī (3.257–3.283)
Mārkaṇḍeya consoles Yudhiṣṭhira with the story of **Rāma** (3.257–3.276) and of **Sāvitrī and Satyavat** (3.277–3.283) — a wife who wins her husband back from Yama.

### A10. The earrings (3.284–3.294)
* **Sūrya's dream-warning (3.284–3.286):** Sūrya appears to Karṇa in a dream as a brahmin: *Indra will come for your armour and earrings — do not give them; your life depends on them.* Karṇa: *if Indra himself begs, I cannot refuse — let my fame live.* Sūrya: *then at least get his unfailing spear in exchange.*
* **Karṇa's birth retold (3.287–3.293):** Kuntī's service to Durvāsas; the mantra; Sūrya; the **lidded, well-padded basket released on the Aśva river**; its journey → Carmaṇvatī → Yamunā → Gaṅgā → **Campā**, where **Rādhā** sees it and **Adhiratha** lifts it out (3.292–3.293).
* **The gift (3.294):** at noon, after his prayers, **Indra comes as a brahmin** and asks for the armour and earrings. Karṇa bargains first: **the Vāsavī Śakti, which will kill one enemy and return to Indra** — *"the one you want is protected by Kṛṣṇa"*; and *"let my body not be disfigured"* — Indra: *no scars; you will have your father's radiance* (3.294.26–34). Karṇa takes a sharp blade and **cuts the armour from his living flesh, smiling again and again**; gods and siddhas cry out; divine drums sound, flowers fall; he hands over the **wet** armour and the earrings — hence **Vaikartana** (3.294.35–38). **Indra, laughing, having deceived him yet made him famous, flies away** (3.294.39).

### A11. The Yakṣa (3.295–3.299)
A brahmin's **fire-sticks (araṇī)** catch in the antlers of a deer, which runs off; the brothers chase it far into the forest. Thirsty, Nakula goes to a lake; **a bodiless voice — "answer my questions first"** — he drinks and falls dead; then Sahadeva, Arjuna (who shoots at the voice), Bhīma (3.296). Yudhiṣṭhira arrives, sees his brothers dead, and answers the **Yakṣa's questions** (3.297): *What is faster than wind? The mind. What is the greatest wonder? Every day creatures go to Yama, yet the rest want to live forever.* Offered one brother's life, he chooses **Nakula** — *so that Mādrī too has a living son.* The Yakṣa is **Dharma**, his father; he revives all four and grants: **in the thirteenth year no one will recognise you** (3.298–3.299).

## B. VIRĀṬA PARVA — THE YEAR IN DISGUISE (4.1–4.67)

### B1. Disguises (4.1–4.12)
At **Virāṭa's city (Matsya)**: **Yudhiṣṭhira = Kaṅka**, a brahmin dice-companion, carrying **golden dice that look like beryl** (4.6.1); **Bhīma = Ballava**, a cook and wrestler; **Arjuna = Bṛhannaḍā** — *"I will vow 'I am a eunuch'; these two great bowstring scars cannot be hidden"*, earrings, braided hair, teacher of song and dance to the princess **Uttarā** (4.2.21–27); **Nakula = Granthika**, horse-keeper; **Sahadeva = Tantipāla**, cowherd; **Draupadī = Sairandhrī**, maid to Queen **Sudeṣṇā**. Weapons are wrapped and tied in a **śamī tree near a cremation ground**, with a corpse hung beside them to keep people away (4.5). `[NOT CE]` Yudhiṣṭhira's hymn to Durgā (App. I.4).

### B2. Kīcaka (4.13–4.23)
**Kīcaka**, the queen's brother and commander, lusts after Sairandhrī; the queen sends her to fetch wine from his house (4.14). She flees into the royal hall; **Kīcaka kicks her before King Virāṭa and the disguised Yudhiṣṭhira**; Bhīma, burning, is restrained by Yudhiṣṭhira's glance (4.15). At night **Draupadī goes secretly to Bhīma in the kitchen**, wakes him, pours out her humiliation (4.16–4.20). A trap: she promises Kīcaka a meeting in the **dark dance-hall**; Bhīma waits on the bed and **crushes Kīcaka into a shapeless ball of flesh** (4.21). The **Upakīcakas** (his 105 brothers) seize Sairandhrī to burn her on his pyre; Bhīma uproots a tree and kills them all (4.22–4.23).

### B3. The cattle raid (4.24–4.62)
* Duryodhana's spies report Kīcaka's death — perhaps Bhīma's work. **Suśarman of Trigarta** raids Virāṭa's southern cattle; Virāṭa is captured, Bhīma (Ballava) frees him (4.29–4.33).
* Meanwhile **the Kaurava host — Bhīṣma, Droṇa, Kṛpa, Karṇa, Aśvatthāman, Duryodhana — raids from the north** (4.33–4.34). Prince **Uttara** boasts in the harem; Bṛhannaḍā becomes his charioteer; **Princess Uttarā asks for the Kauravas' fine cloths for her dolls** (4.35). At the sight of the army Uttara jumps off and runs; Bṛhannaḍā drags him back, makes him charioteer, takes the weapons from the śamī tree and **reveals himself** with his **ten names** — Arjuna, Phalguna, Jiṣṇu, Kirīṭin, Śvetavāhana, Bībhatsu, Vijaya, Kṛṣṇa, Savyasācin, Dhanaṃjaya (4.39–4.40). *"I wore the eunuch's guise only for a year at my elder's command."*
* Duryodhana claims the 13th year is not over; **Bhīṣma calculates the lunar intercalary months: five months and twelve nights more than thirteen years have passed** (4.47).
* **Arjuna alone** defeats Karṇa (who flees), Droṇa, Aśvatthāman, Kṛpa, Bhīṣma and Duryodhana; finally the **sammohana** weapon stupefies all; he tells Uttara to take **Droṇa's and Kṛpa's white cloths, Karṇa's yellow, Aśvatthāman's and Duryodhana's blue — avoid Bhīṣma, who knows the counter** (4.61).
* **The dice and the blood (4.63–4.64):** Virāṭa boasts of his son; Kaṅka says *"with Bṛhannaḍā as charioteer, how could he not win?"*; furious, **Virāṭa strikes Kaṅka's face with the dice; blood flows from his nose — Sairandhrī catches it in a golden bowl** so it never touches the ground (4.63.44–47); Yudhiṣṭhira keeps Bṛhannaḍā out of the room — *"if he sees my blood he will kill the king"* (4.63.54).
* **Revelation & marriage (4.65–4.67):** on the third day the Pāṇḍavas appear in royal splendour; Virāṭa offers Uttarā to Arjuna; **Arjuna accepts her as daughter-in-law** — *"I was her teacher; she is like a daughter"* — for **Abhimanyu**; Kṛṣṇa, the Vṛṣṇis and Pāñcālas come to **Upaplavya** for the wedding.

## C. UDYOGA PARVA — PEACE THAT FAILS (5.1–5.197)

### C1. The council & the choice (5.1–5.8)
* At Virāṭa's court Kṛṣṇa proposes an envoy for peace; Balarāma blames Yudhiṣṭhira's gambling; Sātyaki rebukes Balarāma; Drupada sends his priest to Hastinapura (5.1–5.6).
* **Kṛṣṇa's choice (5.7):** Duryodhana and Arjuna reach Dvārakā the same day; Kṛṣṇa is asleep; **Duryodhana sits at his head, Arjuna stands at his feet with folded hands**. Kṛṣṇa wakes, sees Arjuna first, gives the younger first choice: *one army of a hundred million Nārāyaṇa cowherd warriors equal to me — or I alone, who will not fight.* **Arjuna chooses Kṛṣṇa**; Duryodhana happily takes the army. Balarāma stays neutral.
* **Śalya (5.8):** marching to the Pāṇḍavas, Śalya is lavishly entertained on the road by Duryodhana (whom he thought was Yudhiṣṭhira) and grants him a boon — his army. He then visits Yudhiṣṭhira, who asks one thing: *if you drive Karṇa's chariot, break his spirit* (tejovadha). (Śalya then tells Indra's ordeals — Vṛtra, Nahuṣa, 5.9–5.18.)

### C2. Sañjaya's embassy (5.20–5.32)
Drupada's priest pleads in Hastinapura; Dhṛtarāṣṭra sends **Sañjaya** to Upaplavya. Yudhiṣṭhira's minimum: **"Give us Kuśasthala, Vṛkasthala, Āsandī, Vāraṇāvata and any fifth — five villages for five brothers, Suyodhana, and let there be peace"** (5.31.19–20).

### C3. A sleepless king (5.33–5.69)
Dhṛtarāṣṭra cannot sleep; **Vidura's counsel (Vidura-nīti, 5.33–5.40)**; the sage **Sanatsujāta** on death and immortality (5.41–5.45). Sañjaya delivers Arjuna's message — *Kṛṣṇa and I are Nara-Nārāyaṇa* — and the court quarrels; Duryodhana: *I will not give even as much land as a needle's point can pierce* (5.57.18).

### C4. Draupadī's hair (5.70–5.83)
Before Kṛṣṇa leaves as envoy, each Pāṇḍava speaks — even **Bhīma speaks for peace**, and Kṛṣṇa taunts him into fury. **Draupadī (5.80):** dark, holding her **long, black, fragrant, serpent-like hair in her left hand**, eyes full of tears: *"Remember this hair dragged by Duḥśāsana's hand, Kṛṣṇa, whenever you seek peace. If Bhīma and Arjuna have grown weak, my old father and my five sons, led by Abhimanyu, will fight the Kurus."* Kṛṣṇa: *the Bhāratas' wives will weep as you weep* (5.80).

### C5. Kṛṣṇa in Hastinapura (5.84–5.137)
* He refuses Duryodhana's banquet and **stays with Vidura**; meets **Kuntī**, who weeps for her sons (5.88–5.89).
* **The great court (5.92–5.127):** Kṛṣṇa's speech for peace; parables — Paraśurāma's **Dambhodbhava** (5.94), Kaṇva's **Mātali** (5.95–5.103), Nārada's **Gālava** (5.104–5.121); Bhīṣma, Droṇa, Vidura, Dhṛtarāṣṭra and **Gāndhārī** (brought into the hall, 5.127) plead with Duryodhana. He walks out: **"Not even as much land as the point of a sharp needle pierces, Mādhava"** (5.125.26).
* **The plot & the form (5.128–5.129):** Duryodhana, Karṇa, Śakuni and Duḥśāsana plan to **seize Kṛṣṇa**; Sātyaki warns the court. Kṛṣṇa laughs: *you think I am alone?* — **flames with smoke burst from his eyes, nose and ears**, gods and heroes appear on his body; the kings shut their eyes; **only Droṇa, Bhīṣma, Vidura, Sañjaya and the sages receive divine sight** (5.129.11–13); drums sound, the earth trembles (5.129.14–15). `[NOT CE]` Dhṛtarāṣṭra receiving sight to see the form (*495–*496).
* **Kuntī's message (5.130–5.137):** the story of **Vidulā** urging her defeated son to rise (5.131–5.134); *tell Arjuna and Bhīma: the time has come for which a Kṣatriya woman bears sons.*

### C6. Karṇa's two choices (5.138–5.144)
* **Kṛṣṇa & Karṇa (5.138–5.141):** Kṛṣṇa takes Karṇa in his chariot outside the city: *you are Kuntī's eldest son — the Pāṇḍavas will serve you, Draupadī will come to you, you will be king.* Karṇa refuses: Rādhā and Adhiratha raised him; Duryodhana gave him everything; he foresees the war as a great **sacrifice** in which he and the Kauravas will be offered — and describes the terrible portents.
* **Kuntī & Karṇa (5.142–5.144):** on the Gaṅgā bank, at his noon prayer, Kuntī reveals herself; a voice from the Sun confirms her words. Karṇa: *you abandoned me; now you come for your sons' sake.* But: **"I will not kill your sons Yudhiṣṭhira, Bhīma, Nakula and Sahadeva, though I could; only Arjuna and I will fight. Your sons will always be five — with Arjuna, or with Karṇa"** (5.144.20–22).

### C7. Armies & commanders (5.145–5.169)
* The Pāṇḍavas raise **7 akṣauhiṇīs**, the Kauravas **11**. Pāṇḍava commander: **Dhṛṣṭadyumna** (5.149–5.154); Kaurava commander: **Bhīṣma** (5.153): *"Only Arjuna equals me… but Pāṇḍu's sons I will not destroy; instead I will kill **ten thousand (ayuta) warriors every day**"* (5.153.18–21); condition: *"let Karṇa fight first, or I"* (5.153.24) — Karṇa: **"While Gāṅgeya lives I will not fight; when Bhīṣma falls I will fight the Gāṇḍīva-bearer"** (5.153.25). **Balarāma leaves on pilgrimage** (5.154); **Rukmin** is rejected by both sides (5.155).
* **Ulūka's insults (5.157–5.161):** Duryodhana sends Śakuni's son Ulūka with taunts; Arjuna, Bhīma, Sahadeva reply with renewed vows.
* **Ratha-atiratha (5.162–5.169):** Bhīṣma ranks the warriors; he calls **Karṇa only an "ardharatha" (half a chariot-warrior)** (5.165.11); Karṇa lays down his weapons: *while you live, I will not fight* (5.165).

### C8. Ambā (5.170–5.193)
Bhīṣma tells why he will not fight **Śikhaṇḍin**: Ambā, rejected by Śālva and by Bhīṣma, wanders; **Paraśurāma fights Bhīṣma for 23 days** on her behalf and stops; Ambā does terrible austerity, is promised by Śiva she will kill Bhīṣma in her next life, and **enters fire**; reborn as Drupada's daughter **Śikhaṇḍinī**, raised as a son, she later exchanges sex with the yakṣa **Sthūṇākarṇa** and becomes **Śikhaṇḍin**.

### C9. To Kurukṣetra (5.194–5.197)
Both hosts march to **Kurukṣetra** and camp on opposite sides of the **Hiraṇvatī** river; positions, trenches, supply camps.
